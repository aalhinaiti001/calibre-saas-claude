# Cost of a poor senior (finance) hire, cost of vacancy / re-hire, and GCC mid-market buying behaviour for professional services

Research date: 2026-10-05. Provenance ratings used on every number:
- **[PRIMARY]**: the number comes from the original study, survey or legal/official text, and the text was read here (full text or an extract from the publisher's own file).
- **[SECONDARY]**: a credible outlet citing a primary source, or a primary source whose own basis is a citation to someone else. The original was not read here, or the publisher only asserts the figure.
- **[UNSUPPORTED]**: no primary source can be traced. Treat as a folk statistic.

Access note: americanprogress.org, gallup.com and rec.uk.com blocked direct fetching from this environment. Their documents were read through a full-text retrieval service (Exa) that returns the publisher's own PDF or page text at the publisher's URL. They count as primary reads.

## 1. Credible, primary-sourced estimates of the cost of a bad hire or early turnover (manager/senior, finance where possible)

### Takeaway
Very few studies measure the cost of a *bad hire*. Most of the literature measures the cost of *turnover/replacement*, and the best-documented estimates differ by an order of magnitude:
- Center for American Progress (CAP) 2012 meta-review: typical (median) cost about 21% of salary; up to 213% for executives.
- Oxford Economics 2014: about £30.6k per replaced £25k+ professional, and about £39k in accountancy.
- REC 2017: an *illustrative* worked example of about 3.1x salary for a £42k audit/middle-manager mis-hire.
- CareerBuilder 2017: self-reported average loss of $14,900 per bad hire.
- Gallup 2019: "0.5x to 2x salary", asserted without a stated method.

A defensible pricing rationale should quote ranges with their method attached and should not rely on any single multiplier.

### Cited Findings

**Center for American Progress: Boushey & Glynn, "There Are Significant Business Costs to Replacing Employees" (16 Nov 2012)** [PRIMARY]
- Method: a literature review/meta-synthesis of "30 case studies taken from the 11 most-relevant research papers", published 1992–2007. It is not new data collection. It measures *turnover* cost (lost productivity at exit, hiring and training costs, slower productivity while the new hire ramps up). It does not measure bad-hire cost specifically. — [CAP PDF](https://www.americanprogress.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/11/CostofTurnover.pdf)
- Median cost of turnover is 21% of annual salary for all positions except executives and physicians (27 case studies). The figure shows 21.4% for all cases and 20.7% excluding physicians and executives. — [CAP PDF](https://www.americanprogress.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/11/CostofTurnover.pdf)
- For jobs paying under $50k the median is 20% (22 case studies). For jobs paying $30k or less it is 16% (16.1% in the figure). For jobs paying $75k or less it is 20.4%. — [CAP PDF](https://www.americanprogress.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/11/CostofTurnover.pdf)
- Across all 30 cases the range is 5.8% to 213%. "Very highly paid jobs and those at the senior or executive levels tend to have disproportionately high turnover costs as a percentage of salary (up to 213 percent)". Among jobs at $75k or less, 17 case studies fall between 10% and 30%. — [CAP PDF](https://www.americanprogress.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/11/CostofTurnover.pdf)
- Caveat: the underlying studies date from 1992–2007. Only 3 of the 30 cases are executive or physician roles. The 213% upper bound comes from very few data points.

**Oxford Economics for Unum, "The Cost of Brain Drain" (Feb/Mar 2014)**. This is the most finance-relevant study because accountancy is one of its five sectors. [PRIMARY]
- Method: a bespoke survey of UK firms plus Labour Force Survey data. It covers 5 sectors (IT/Tech, Accounting, Legal, Media/Advertising, Retail) and workers earning £25,000+. It measures the cost of *replacing a departing employee*. — [Oxford Economics page, 3 Mar 2014](https://www.oxfordeconomics.com/resource/the-cost-of-brain-drain/); [Report PDF](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf)
- The average cost per departing £25k+ employee is £30,614, ranging from £20,113 in retail to £39,887 in legal. — [Report PDF](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf)
- Components:
  - Lost output while the new worker reaches "optimal productivity" is £25,181 on average (£16,240 retail to £35,307 legal). It is made up of about £13,128 in "lost wages" and about £12,054 in lost capital income.
  - Logistical cost averages £5,433. The executive summary puts the upper end at £6,630 in accountancy. A later section says £6,455 in IT/Tech is the upper end. This is an internal inconsistency in the report.
  — [Report PDF](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf)
- Logistics breakdown [SECONDARY, from press coverage of the report]:
  - temporary cover £3,618
  - management interview time £767
  - agency fees £454
  - advertising £398
  - HR processing £196
  — [Corporate Adviser, 3 Mar 2014](https://corporate-adviser.com/30k-cost-of-replacing-employee/); [theHRD, 24 Feb 2014](https://www.thehrdirector.com/business-news/financial/the-cost-of-replacing-a-25k-per-annum-employee/)
- **Accountancy**: the overall cost of losing an employee is "just short of £40,000" in accounting and legal. Most of the excess reflects lost capital income, meaning value that accrues to owners or partners. — [Report PDF](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf). Press coverage gives £39,230 for accountancy and 32 weeks to optimum productivity [SECONDARY] — [Telegraph, 24 Feb 2014](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/finance/jobs/10657008/Replacing-staff-costs-British-businesses-4bn-each-year.html); [HRZone, 13 Mar 2014](https://hrzone.com/blog/how-much-does-staff-turnover-really-cost-you/)
- Weeks of lost output in **Accounting** by the new hire's background: same sector 7.4, other sector 20.1, university leaver 21.3, previously unemployed or inactive 32.2. — [Report PDF, Table 1](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf)
- Time to optimal productivity, all sectors: 28 weeks on average. Same-sector hires take 15 weeks, other-sector hires 32 weeks, graduates 40 weeks, previously unemployed 52 weeks. SMEs (1–250 staff) take 24 weeks and micro firms 12 weeks. — [Report PDF](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf); [theHRD](https://www.thehrdirector.com/business-news/financial/the-cost-of-replacing-a-25k-per-annum-employee/)
- **Vacancy cover in accountancy**: 14% of accounting employers reported temp costs above £400/day. The accountancy and IT sectors spend "over £4,000 per vacancy" on temporary workers. The average temp day costs about £150 in retail and about £70 more in accounting and IT. — [Report PDF](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf)
- Caveat: an independent mental-health costing paper notes the figure applies only to £25k+ earners in five sectors that "may not be representative of the economy as a whole". — [Centre for Mental Health / infocop PDF](https://www.infocop.es/pdf/MENTALHEALTHWORK.pdf)

**REC (Recruitment & Employment Confederation) with Indeed, "Perfect Match: Making the right hire and the cost of getting it wrong" (press release 21 Jun 2017; report page dated 20 Jul 2017)** [PRIMARY]
- Method:
  - An online survey of HR decision-makers.
  - Press coverage gives the sample as "an April poll of 500 HR staff" plus interviews with agency bosses [SECONDARY for the sample size]. — [ContractorUK, 23 Jun 2017](https://www.contractoruk.com/news/0013068hiring_blunders_costing_uk_plc_billions.html)
  - The methodology appendix (p.43) was not retrieved in full here.
- 85% of respondents say a business they worked for hired the wrong person. Respondents named the biggest costs as training (53%), morale/performance (46%), recruitment (41%) and lost productivity (36%). — [REC report PDF](https://www.rec.uk.com/download_file/2883/6683)
- **£132,015 for a £42,000 middle manager.** This is an *illustrative worked scenario*, not a measured average:
  - "Company Z" (80 employees) hires "Employee A" into "a team of five audit professionals" at £42,000.
  - The firm fires A after 8 months, and the replacement starts 5 months later.
  - The team works overtime during the vacancy.
  - A senior team member then quits.
  - The report says the figure "will vary according to basic salary, period of employment and position".
  — [REC report PDF](https://www.rec.uk.com/download_file/2883/6683); [REC press release](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/news/press-releases/hiring-mistakes-are-costing-uk-businesses-billions-each-year-rec)
- The REC foreword says: "for a middle manager, the cost of a bad hire can be over three times their salary" (£132,015 / £42,000 = 3.14x). — [REC report PDF](https://www.rec.uk.com/download_file/2883/6683)
- Self-reported costs: 10% of respondents whose business made a poor *senior-management* hire estimate it cost £100,000 or more in the last year; for middle-management hires the figure is 3%. 33% say a senior bad hire cost nothing, and about a fifth answer "don't know". — [REC report PDF](https://www.rec.uk.com/download_file/2883/6683)
- The REC's "billions" national estimate rests on a Leadership IQ claim that 40% of hires fail within 18 months, which is a vendor claim [SECONDARY/weak]. It also assumes each bad hire stays one month at average weekly earnings of £539. — [REC press release](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/news/press-releases/hiring-mistakes-are-costing-uk-businesses-billions-each-year-rec)

**CareerBuilder / Harris Poll (press release 7 Dec 2017)** [PRIMARY for the survey result, but self-reported]
- Companies lost an average of $14,900 per bad hire in the last year, and 74% of employers say they have hired the wrong person.
- Method: an online Harris Poll, 16 Aug–15 Sep 2017. The sample was 2,257 full-time US private-sector hiring managers and HR professionals plus 3,697 workers.
- Impacts named: lower productivity 37%, time to recruit/train a replacement 32%, lower quality of work 31%.
- Losing a *good* hire was reported to cost about $30,000 on average.
— [PR Newswire, 7 Dec 2017](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nearly-three-in-four-employers-affected-by-a-bad-hire-according-to-a-recent-careerbuilder-survey-300567056.html); [HR Dive, 8 Dec 2017](https://www.hrdive.com/news/careerbuilder-74-of-employers-admit-hiring-the-wrong-candidate/512577/); [HR Daily Advisor](https://hrdailyadvisor.hci.org/2017/12/19/anatomy-costs-bad-hire/)

**SHRM: recruiting-cost benchmarks (measured), and the "50–200%" turnover range (cited, not measured)**
- *SHRM Benchmarking: Talent Access Report* (launched as a member benefit in Apr 2022) [PRIMARY]:
  - Cost-per-hire: average $4,683, median $1,244 (n=472).
  - **Executive cost-per-hire: average $28,329, median $8,750, 75th percentile $35,000 (n=212).**
  - Cost-per-hire covers recruiting costs only: agency fees, advertising, job boards, referrals, travel, relocation, recruiter pay, and talent-acquisition systems.

- Earlier *SHRM Talent Acquisition Customized Benchmarking Report* (year not stated on the retrieved copy, about 2016–2017) [PRIMARY, undated]: cost-per-hire average $4,425 (n=488); executive cost-per-hire average $14,936 (n=247); time-to-fill average 36 days, median 30 (n=864). — [copy hosted by farmerlawpc.com](https://farmerlawpc.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Talent-Acquisition-Report-All-Industries-All-FTEs.pdf)
- SHRM article "The Real Costs of Recruitment" (2022). It says "nearly $4,700" cost per hire. It also says "many employers estimate the total cost to hire a new employee can be three to four times the position's salary, according to Edie Goldberg", a talent consultant. **This is a consultant's opinion, not SHRM data** [SECONDARY/opinion]. — [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/real-costs-recruitment)
- SHRM Foundation, David G. Allen, *Retaining Talent* (2008; outside the 2012–2026 window, included for provenance only). It says "direct replacement costs can reach as high as 50%–60% of an employee's annual salary, with total costs associated with turnover ranging from 90% to 200%". The endnote cites Cascio's 2006 HR **textbook** and a 2001 Academy of Management Executive article. It gives an example turnover cost of "$150,000 for an accounting professional". [SECONDARY: SHRM is citing others] — [Retaining Talent text (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/749124358/Retaining-Talent-A-Guide-to-Analyzing-an); [provenance trace, retainscore](https://retainscore.com/provenance/six-to-nine-months-of-salary)

**CIPD Resourcing and Talent Planning surveys (UK)**. These measure recruitment cost per hire only, not bad-hire cost. [PRIMARY]
- 2026 report: median cost per hire is **£3,500 for senior managers/directors** (n=263) and £2,000 for other employees (n=223). The private-sector senior median is £5,000 (n=170). The figures use only respondents with ±20% accuracy, and CIPD warns of small samples. — [CIPD 2026 PDF](https://www.cipd.org/globalassets/media/knowledge/knowledge-hub/reports/2026-pdfs/9218-resource-and-talent-planning-2026-report-web.pdf)
- Time series for the senior manager/director median: £2,000 (2024, n=246), £3,000 (2022, n=265), £5,000 (2020), £6,000 (2017, n=143), £8,000 (2012). — [CIPD 2024 PDF](https://www.cipd.org/globalassets/media/knowledge/knowledge-hub/reports/2024-pdfs/8662-resource-and-talent-planning-2024-report-web.pdf)
- An older CIPD survey (year not shown on the retrieved copy; pre-2012, so outside the window) gave a median labour-turnover cost of £9,000 for senior managers/directors (n=11) and £6,125 for all employees (n=22). **Gallup, McFeely & Wigert, "This Fixable Problem Costs U.S. Businesses $1 Trillion" (13 Mar 2019)** [SECONDARY: Gallup asserts the figure without a stated method]
- "The cost of replacing an individual employee can range from one-half to two times the employee's annual salary — and that's a conservative estimate." The article gives no method or citation for this range.
- Illustration: a 100-person firm with a $50k average salary and the BLS 2017 turnover rate of 26.3% would face about $660k–$2.6m per year.
- Gallup's own survey data in the same piece: 52% of voluntary leavers say their manager or organisation could have prevented the exit; 51% had no conversation about their future in the prior 3 months.
— [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses-trillion.aspx)

### Inferences
- The most finance-specific measured evidence is Oxford Economics 2014 for accountancy: about £39k per replaced £25k+ employee and about 32 weeks to full productivity. The other finance-specific case is REC's *hypothetical* audit-team scenario at about 3.1x salary. For a senior finance hire, these support a value range of roughly **0.2x salary** (CAP median, recruitment and ramp-up only) to **1–3x salary** (scenario models that include vacancy, rework and knock-on attrition). Any figure above that rests on opinion.
- Recruiting spend alone (SHRM executive cost-per-hire about $28k average, CIPD senior median £3.5k) is a small share of the total. Most of the modelled cost is lost output during vacancy and ramp-up, plus rework. The "re-run of the hiring process" therefore costs more than the agency fee.
- Measured surveys of what employers actually lost (CareerBuilder $14.9k, REC self-reports) come out well below the modelled multipliers. Many respondents also say "nothing" or "don't know", which REC itself flags as a measurement gap.

### Gaps
- No primary study isolates the cost of a mis-hired **CFO/finance director/financial controller**. The REC example (audit professional, £42k) and Oxford Economics (accountancy sector, £25k+) are the closest.
- The REC methodology appendix (exact sample size, fieldwork agency) was not read directly. The figure of 500 HR respondents in April 2017 comes from press coverage.
- The Oxford Economics survey sample size was not retrieved.
- No GCC-specific bad-hire or turnover cost study was found.
- No good primary study on the cost of a *prolonged vacancy* per day or week was found, apart from the Oxford Economics temp-cover data and the REC scenario's overtime and lost-productivity lines.

## 2. Provenance of commonly repeated claims

### Takeaway
"A bad hire costs at least 30% of first-year earnings (U.S. Department of Labor)" is **[UNSUPPORTED]**: no DOL or BLS document has ever been traced. "A bad hire costs 3x salary" goes back to an illustrative REC scenario (3.14x) and to consultant opinion quoted by SHRM ("3–4x"). It is **not a measured average**. "50–200% of salary (SHRM)" is SHRM summarising a 2006 textbook, which is a secondary citation.

### Cited Findings
- **"30% of first-year earnings – US DOL" → [UNSUPPORTED]**
  - Many outlets repeat it with no link, e.g. [Forbes, 28 Sep 2016](https://www.forbes.com/sites/falonfatemi/2016/09/28/the-true-cost-of-a-bad-hire-its-more-than-you-think/) and [Prevue HR, 2024](https://www.prevuehr.com/resources/insights/understanding-cost-of-bad-hires/).
  - Some date it "US Dept of Labor 2003" without naming any document, e.g. [Battalia Winston PDF](https://www.battaliawinston.com/pdf/Cost-of-a-bad-hire.pdf).
  - Provenance traces conclude that no DOL or BLS publication contains it. Newman HR reports that "a direct search of dol.gov returns no such figure". It cites an EASI·Consult 2014 article by Rebekah Cardenas that described the figure as spread by "blogs that cite other blogs". The EASI·Consult article was not read here. — [Newman HR](https://newmanhumanresources.com/kb/numbers/numbers-cost-of-bad-hire/); [HireUA, 3 Aug 2026](https://hire-ua.com/cost-of-a-bad-hire/); [Stealth Agents, 12 Sep 2026](https://stealthagents.com/research/true-cost-of-a-bad-hire-2026-statistics-and-benchmarks)

  - The US variant is SHRM's 2022 article quoting consultant Edie Goldberg: "three to four times the position's salary". — [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/real-costs-recruitment)

- **"0.5x–2x salary (Gallup)" → [SECONDARY]**: Gallup asserts it without method. — [Gallup, 13 Mar 2019](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses-trillion.aspx)
- **"SHRM: a bad hire costs 33% of salary" / "SHRM: $240,000"** appear in aggregator blogs with no SHRM document. **[UNSUPPORTED]**. No CIPD publication with this figure was found. **[UNSUPPORTED pending verification]** — [Vantage Search Group, 28 Sep 2026](https://vantagesearchgroup.me/insights/executive-search-fees-uae/); [Gulf Workforce](https://www.gulfworkforce.com/blogs/recruitment-agency-fees-uae)

### Inferences
- A pricing rationale should not cite "DOL 30%" at all. If it needs a "30%" anchor, the CAP finding is defensible: 17 of the cases for jobs at $75k or less fall between 10% and 30%, with a 21% median. That covers turnover replacement cost, not mis-hire cost.
- If it needs a "~3x" anchor, it should say "REC (2017) illustrative scenario: a £42k audit-team middle manager fired at 8 months and replaced, total £132k (~3.1x salary)". It should not present this as a measured average.

### Gaps
- The dol.gov search was not run directly in this session. The finding that no DOL source exists relies on the convergent secondary provenance traces cited above.
- The EASI·Consult (Cardenas 2014) article was not located directly.

## 3. GCC-specific cost mechanics: gratuity on early exit, probation, visa and onboarding fees

### Takeaway
UAE and KSA law makes *early* exit cheap in gratuity terms:
- **UAE**: no gratuity before 1 year of continuous service.
- **KSA**: no award on resignation before 2 years. Employer termination still accrues a pro-rata half-month per year.

Probation of up to 6 months (UAE) or 180 days (KSA, since Feb 2025) lets an employer exit a mis-hire with 14 days' notice (UAE) or terminate during probation (KSA). The sunk cost of an expatriate mis-hire therefore sits mostly in non-refundable government fees and lost output, not in severance:
- **KSA**: about SAR 9,600/yr levy, plus about SAR 2,000 visa, plus iqama and medical.
- **UAE**: AED 250–3,450 work permit plus ICP residency and Emirates ID fees.

### Cited Findings

**UAE: Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 (as amended), official MOHRE text** [PRIMARY]
- Art. 9(1): probation of no more than 6 months. The employer may terminate during probation with at least 14 days' written notice. — [MOHRE PDF](https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/e82f7872/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments_638990571068264034.pdf.aspx)
- Art. 9(2): probation may be applied only once per employer.
- Art. 9(3): a worker who moves to another UAE employer during probation must give at least one month's notice, and "the new employer shall compensate the original employer for the costs of recruitment or contracting with the worker, unless otherwise agreed".
- Art. 9(4): a worker leaving the UAE during probation gives 14 days' notice. If they return on a new work permit within 3 months, the new employer pays that compensation.
- Art. 9(5): a party that breaches the notice rule pays compensation equal to wages for the notice period.
- Art. 9(6): a worker who leaves in breach is barred from a work permit for 1 year.
— [Dubai Digital Authority copy of the law](https://dda.gov.ae/-/media/22A3CF4734034742B62468DB2721BEF1.pdf); [MOHRE PDF](https://mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/9ade07/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments.pdf.aspx)
- Art. 51(2)–(3): a full-time foreign worker "who completed a year or more in continuous service" gets 21 days' basic wage for each of the first 5 years and 30 days for each later year. Part-years are paid pro rata *only once one year is completed*. Art. 51(6): the total is capped at 2 years' wage. Payment is due within 14 days of the end of employment (Art. 53). — [DDA copy](https://dda.gov.ae/-/media/22A3CF4734034742B62468DB2721BEF1.pdf)

- An all-in employer estimate for mainland Category 3 is AED 5,950–12,430, made up of work permit, medical and Emirates ID AED 700–980, and residency and stamping AED 4,000–7,000. **[SECONDARY: law-firm marketing estimate, not official]** — [Labour Lawyer Dubai](https://labourlawyerdubai.ae/blog/employment-visa-charges-in-uae/)

**KSA: Labor Law (Royal Decree M/51, 2005) and Feb 2025 amendments, official MHRSD texts** [PRIMARY]
- Amended Art. 53 (in force Feb 2025): probation "does not exceed one hundred and eighty days in all cases". Either party may terminate during it. Previously the limit was 90 days, extendable to 180 by written agreement. — [MHRSD Amendments to Labor Law Articles (Mar 2025)](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-03/Amendments%20to%20Labor%20Law%20Articles.pdf); [Pinsent Masons, 7 Apr 2025, SECONDARY](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/saudi-arabia-labor-law-amendments-fair-treatment-employment); [Dentons, Sep 2024, SECONDARY](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/alerts/2024/september/18/amendments-to-the-kingdom-of-saudi-arabia-labor-law-key-changes-for-employers-in-the-ksa-for-2025)
- Art. 84: the end-of-service award is a half-month wage for each of the first 5 years and a one-month wage for each later year, based on last wage, with part-years paid pro rata. — [MHRSD Labor Law PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2023-02/Labor.pdf)
- Art. 85: on **resignation**, the worker gets one-third of the award for 2–5 years of service, two-thirds for 5–10 years, and the full award for 10+ years. A worker who resigns with under 2 years gets nothing. — [MHRSD Labor Law PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2023-02/Labor.pdf)
- New Art. 79 (resignation process): resignation is deemed accepted after 30 days without a response. The employer may defer acceptance for up to 60 days. Resignation is filed via Qiwa. — [MHRSD amendments PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-03/Amendments%20to%20Labor%20Law%20Articles.pdf)
- Notice for indefinite contracts is now 30 days from the employee and 60 days from the employer [SECONDARY]. — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/saudi-arabia-labor-law-amendments-fair-treatment-employment)

**KSA: expatriate fees**
- MHRSD "financial equivalent" (expat levy) [PRIMARY but dated Nov 2021, outside the 2024–2026 window]:
  - **SAR 800/month (SAR 9,600/yr)** for each foreign worker above the number of Saudi workers.
  - **SAR 700/month (SAR 8,400/yr)** where foreign workers do not exceed Saudis.
  - Plus a work-licence fee of SAR 100/yr.
  - Payable quarterly, semi-annually or annually.
  - The English version prints "9,100" annual for the 800 tier. This is a typo: the Arabic version and 12 × 800 both give 9,600.
  — [MHRSD English PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2021-11/07032022.pdf); [MHRSD Arabic PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2021-11/24112021.pdf); [MHRSD Issue/Renew Work Licence service page (SAR 100)](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/en/ministry-services/services/%D8%A5%D8%B5%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%88-%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D8%B1%D8%AE%D8%B5-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84)
- 2026 secondary sources say these rates still apply (SAR 800/700; SAR 100 licence). They also report a **December 2025 exemption for establishments holding an Industrial Licence**, not yet confirmed in official gazette text. They report a small-enterprise exemption (≤9 employees, Saudi owner registered with GOSI: 2 or 4 levy-free expats) extended to March 2027. **[SECONDARY]** — [CalcMENA (figures "as of May 2026")](https://calcmena.com/ksa-expat-levy-calculator); [Wathim (June 2026)](https://www.wathim.com/services/work-permit/saudi-arabia)
- Iqama issuance or renewal is about **SAR 650/yr** for a private-sector worker, plus a SAR 51.75 Absher processing fee (Jan 2025). A work-visa authorisation is about **SAR 2,000** (some sources cite SAR 2,000–7,000 depending on profession and nationality). The first sponsor transfer fee is SAR 2,000 (SAR 4,000 for the second, SAR 6,000 for the third and later). Iqama medical is about SAR 150–500. **[SECONDARY: no official 2024–2026 fee page retrieved]** — [Wathim Iqama](https://www.wathim.com/services/residency-visa/saudi-arabia); [Noble Core, 18 Jun 2026](https://noblecoreksa.com/work-visa-saudi-arabia/); [Aspirock calculator](https://www.aspirock.com/tools/saudi-arabia-employment-cost-calculator)
- **Finance-role specific**: accountancy Saudisation is 40% of accounting staff from Oct 2025, rising to 70% by 2030. Firms report raising Saudi salaries to retain staff. PIF-linked project directors' pay starts around SAR 80,000/month. **[SECONDARY: journalism quoting firm managers; the official MHRSD decision was not retrieved]** — [AGBI, 18 Mar 2025](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/employment/2025/03/smes-struggle-as-skills-shortages-drive-up-saudi-salaries/)

### Inferences
- **UAE mis-hire exited within probation (up to 6 months).** Gratuity is zero (the 1-year threshold is not met). Notice is 14 days. Fees already paid are sunk and not refunded: MOHRE permit AED 250–3,450 plus ICP and Emirates ID fees. The new hire then needs a fresh permit and residency cycle. If the *employee* leaves during probation for another UAE employer, Art. 9(3) provides for recovering recruitment costs from the new employer. That is a partial offset not available in the UK or US studies.
- **KSA mis-hire.** If terminated within the 180-day probation, Art. 84 itself still says the award accrues pro rata. However, Art. 80/probation termination treatment and the Implementing Regulations were not reviewed. If terminated by the employer after probation, a pro-rata half-month per year is due. If the employee resigns before 2 years, no award is due. The levy (SAR 8,400–9,600/yr, often prepaid for 3–12 months), visa (about SAR 2,000) and iqama (about SAR 650) are the main sunk expat-specific costs, so a re-hire roughly doubles them in year one.
- Gratuity and severance make up a small part of the cost of a GCC mis-hire. The value case rests on lost output during vacancy and ramp-up (Oxford Economics: 7–32 weeks lost in accountancy depending on the hire's background), rework, and a second search fee.

### Gaps
- An official KSA 2024–2026 fee page for the visa (SAR 2,000), iqama (SAR 650) and the Dec 2025 industrial exemption was not retrieved. These are secondary only.
- No official UAE medical-fitness fee (DHA/EHS) was retrieved.
- The KSA Art. 77 compensation for unjustified termination and the Art. 80 rules were not retrieved, nor whether the end-of-service award applies to termination *during* probation.
- The official MHRSD decision text for the accounting Saudisation quota was not retrieved.
- The UAE MOHRE probation text quoted is from the amended consolidated law. The exact amendment date of the PDF version was not confirmed.

## 4. How GCC SME and family-business buyers purchase professional services

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on GCC **mid-market/SME** buying behaviour for professional services is thin.
- The best survey data (Source Global Research) covers **large** buyers (94% at organisations with revenue over $500m). It shows price and speed weigh more in the GCC than elsewhere, alongside strong downward price pressure.
- Qualitative evidence on smaller firms (Botho, 2021) shows budget-led buying at operating level. When the board is involved, buyers prefer brand-name firms.
- UAE accounting-service price lists show that fixed monthly or annual packages at round AED price points, quoted "+ VAT", are the norm in the SME segment. This is vendor evidence, not buyer research.
- UAE VAT law requires AED amounts on tax invoices even when the invoice is in a foreign currency.
- No study was found on approval thresholds or round-number price-point effects.

### Cited Findings

  - GCC clients rank thought-leadership quality 3rd among attributes, against 7th globally.
  - "Value" is measured as perceived multiples of fees (1x/2x/5x/10x).

### Inferences
- For a UAE-registered supplier, pricing in USD is legally possible, but every VAT invoice must also carry AED amounts and the exchange rate. Quoting in AED (or SAR in KSA) removes friction for the buyer's finance team.
- Local SME competitors price in round AED numbers on fixed packages, a pattern observed in vendor price lists. A USD price converted to an odd AED amount will look unfamiliar against them. This is an inference from market convention, not from buyer research.
- The evidence suggests GCC buyers will pay for demonstrable value and quality. Smaller firms face a credibility discount when the decision goes to the board. A fixed fee framed against a quantified mis-hire cost fits the "measurable value" pattern Source describes, but it has not been tested in mid-market samples.
- Recruiter fees for a senior finance hire in the UAE (about 15–33% of first-year pay) set the reference price a buyer will mentally compare against. On an AED 480k package that is AED 72k–158k.

### Gaps
- **No study was found** that measures GCC SME or family-business price sensitivity, approval/sign-off thresholds (e.g., owner sign-off above AED X), or round-number vs charm-pricing effects for professional services.
- No GCC survey was found on invoice-currency preference (USD vs AED/SAR) among SME buyers.
- No Dubai SME (DED) or Monsha'at publication was found that reports SME spending on professional or advisory services or pricing preferences. Monsha'at SME Monitor issues (Q1 2022, Q4 2022, Q2 2023) discuss advisory and talent support programmes such as Tomoh's "access to professional services" but give no pricing-behaviour data. — [Monsha'at Q1 2022](https://www.monshaat.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2022-06/v6.0%20Monshaat%20Q1%202022%20-%20EN_compressed%20%281%29.pdf); [Monsha'at Q2 2023](https://www.monshaat.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2023-08/Monshaat%20SME%20Monitor%20Q2%202023%20-%20EN.pdf)
- The KSA ZATCA rule on SAR amounts on e-invoices, and the official central-bank USD pegs (AED and SAR), were not retrieved in this session.
- The PwC Middle East Family Business Survey 2023 and 2025 editions are archived or not retrieved, so the latest family-business data is from 2019.

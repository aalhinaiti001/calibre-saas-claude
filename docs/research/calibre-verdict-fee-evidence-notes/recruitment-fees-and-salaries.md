# Recruitment / executive search fees and senior finance salaries: UAE, KSA, Jordan (2025–2026)

Research date: 5 October 2026. Currency pegs used throughout: AED 3.6725 = USD 1; SAR 3.75 = USD 1; JOD 0.709 = USD 1 (so USD = JOD ÷ 0.709).
Source-type labels used below: **[SURVEY]** = recruiter salary guide or survey with a stated method; **[CONTRACT]** = a recruiter's published terms of business (primary document); **[MARKETING]** = recruiter or vendor blog or marketing claim, with no survey method disclosed; **[AGGREGATOR]** = salary-aggregator website with a modelled or crowd-sourced method.

## Q1. What are typical contingency and retained executive search fees in the GCC, and do UAE, KSA and Jordan differ?

### Takeaway
No independent survey of GCC recruitment fee levels was found. The evidence is (a) recruiters' own terms of business and (b) many recruiter marketing blogs. Together they show a consistent picture:
- **Contingency:** roughly 15–25% of first-year remuneration for manager-to-director hires. Some blogs quote as low as 10–12%.
- **Retained executive search:** roughly 20–35% of first-year total compensation, with 25–33% the most common quote. It is usually invoiced in three equal stages: engagement, shortlist and placement.

KSA quotes are the same as UAE quotes. No Jordan-specific fee percentage was found.

### Cited Findings
**Primary contract documents [CONTRACT]**
- Robert Walters Dubai Limited, retained terms of business (Dubai and GCC; "Issued March 2021"; the file name is prefixed 240807, which suggests an Aug 2024 re-issue). Retained Search Fees are "a percentage of the Salary Package", agreed per mandate. If no rate is agreed, RW's "standard percentages" apply, but the version published online has the fee table removed ("no fee table").
  - Invoicing is in three stages: one-third on RW agreeing to the mandate; one-third on shortlist presentation or after 30 days, whichever is sooner; and the balance on the candidate's first day, adjusted to the final package.
  - Retained fees are "non-refundable".
  - The "Territory" covered by the terms explicitly includes Jordan, Saudi Arabia and the UAE.
  - The anti-poaching clause charges "fifty percent (33%)" of Salary Package. This is a drafting inconsistency in the source.
  - [RW retained ToB PDF](https://www.robertwalters.ae/content/dam/robert-walters-redesign/country/middle-east/files/terms-of-business/240807%20RW%20Dubai%20Limited%20TOB%20Retained%20(no%20fee%20table).pdf)
- Huxley Associates Global Ltd (Dubai/DIFC branch), permanent recruitment terms (undated):
  - Clause 6 states the fee "is calculated as 35% of the anticipated first year's total remuneration".
  - The billing clause then says the 1st instalment is 50% of the fee, due when the shortlisted candidate signs the offer. A reconciling 2nd invoice brings the total "to a point where the combined amounts are equivalent to 18% of the candidate's total anticipated first year remuneration package". This is internally inconsistent (35% headline against an 18% reconciled total). On a literal reading the effective fee is 18%.
  - The guarantee is a free replacement if the hire leaves within 12 weeks.
  - Executive or retained search fees are "agreed on a case by case basis".
  - [Huxley UAE terms PDF](https://www.huxley.com/media/cati1rni/6-v1-01-uae-huxley-retained-perm-recruitment-tc.pdf)
- DIFC Small Claims Tribunal, *Lukyini Middle East FZ v Listiyana* [2020] DIFC SCT 290 (25 Oct 2020; **2020 data**). This is a recruitment-fee dispute over a doctor whose guaranteed income was AED 35,000–40,000/month. The agency's invoice was AED 100,800 and the employer argued it should be AED 25,000. The court upheld the full AED 100,800 because the fee schedule tied the fee to guaranteed fixed income. The fee percentage itself is not reported. [Legal Wires case summary](https://legal-wires.com/uae-difc-cases/sct-2902020-lukyini-middle-east-fz-llc-v-listiyana-2020-difc-s-20201025/)

**Jordan**
- Alliance Recruitment Agency (Jordan page, undated): "Our fee is a percentage of the hire's first-year salary … payable when they start. No retainer for standard permanent placements". No percentage is stated. [Alliance](https://www.alliancerecruitmentagency.com/jordan-recruitment-agency/)

### Inferences
- The modal quotes are about 20% contingency for Finance Manager and Financial Controller hires, and about 25–33% retained for Head of Finance, Finance Director and CFO hires. These are recruiter-stated ranges, not measured averages.
- The three-stage retainer (one-third at each stage) is a published contractual practice in the GCC (Robert Walters), not only a blog claim.
- KSA quotes do not differ materially from UAE quotes in the sources found.
- Robert Walters' GCC terms cover Jordan as part of the same "Territory", which suggests multinational firms apply the same commercial model there. That is an inference from the scope of the terms, not evidence of Jordanian fee levels.

### Gaps
- No independent survey of GCC agency fee levels was found (e.g. from AESC, CIPD or a trade body). The "12.5% median" figure appears only in recruiter blogs, and its claimed CIPD source could not be verified.
- No GCC recruiter's published fee table with actual percentages was found. Robert Walters publishes its terms with the fee table removed, and the Huxley terms contradict themselves.
- **Minimum fees:** no published GCC minimum fee (AED/SAR/USD floor) was found.
- **Jordan:** no published fee percentage, minimum fee or retainer structure was found.
- The Cooper Fitch (cooperfitch.ae) and hrnow.me domains were blocked by the network proxy. Cooper Fitch PDFs were read through a third-party fetch tool, and the Cooper Fitch 2026 guides (gated download) could not be read.

## Q3. Are housing and transport allowances included in the base on which GCC search fees are calculated?

### Takeaway
Generally yes, in the published contracts. Both GCC terms of business found calculate the fee on total first-year remuneration, explicitly including housing, car and other allowances, plus guaranteed bonus and joining incentives. Some recruiter blogs describe a "base salary" basis and advise employers to negotiate allowances out, so the basis is negotiable in practice.

### Cited Findings
- **Robert Walters GCC retained terms [CONTRACT]**: "Salary Package" means "the annual anticipated gross remuneration package … which includes gross annual salary, applicable benefits for which there is a cash equivalent paid to the Candidate, profit share, commission, bonus, living allowances, travel allowances, overseas allowances, joining inducements and UAE Nationals Child and Social allowances". A company car is "valued at 30,000 AED … per annum" for fee purposes. [RW retained ToB](https://www.robertwalters.ae/content/dam/robert-walters-redesign/country/middle-east/files/terms-of-business/240807%20RW%20Dubai%20Limited%20TOB%20Retained%20(no%20fee%20table).pdf)
- **Huxley Dubai terms [CONTRACT]**: the fee is on "total remuneration … including, but not limited to any guaranteed bonus or housing allowance, car allowance, weighting allowance, all other allowances, profit share and sign on fees". [Huxley UAE terms](https://www.huxley.com/media/cati1rni/6-v1-01-uae-huxley-retained-perm-recruitment-tc.pdf)
- **Mystic Sourcing (10 Sep 2026) [MARKETING]**: percentages are "calculated based on the candidate's annual base salary (excluding bonuses and allowances, though some agencies may negotiate to include guaranteed allowances)". It advises clients to ensure the fee base is "basic salary or basic + housing" and excludes one-off bonuses, schooling allowances and flight tickets. [Mystic Sourcing](https://mysticsourcing.com/blog/recruitment-agency-cost-uae)
- **ReapHR [MARKETING]**: contingency is quoted on "base salary", while retained is quoted on "first-year total compensation". [ReapHR](https://reaphr.com/blog/executive-search-vs-recruitment-gcc-employers-guide)
- **DIFC SCT 290/2020 [court decision; 2020]**: the fee followed the "guaranteed" fixed monthly income named in the offer letter, not variable commission. [Legal Wires](https://legal-wires.com/uae-difc-cases/sct-2902020-lukyini-middle-east-fz-llc-v-listiyana-2020-difc-s-20201025/)

  - Charterhouse 2026: "basic, housing & transport". [Charterhouse PDF](https://www.charterhouseme.ae/rails/active_storage/blobs/redirect/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsibWVzc2FnZSI6IkJBaHBBMDhPTXc9PSIsImV4cCI6bnVsbCwicHVyIjoiYmxvYl9pZCJ9fQ==--b4476eff4af3817f68d90ad0da4da7d1980461d1/UAE%20Salary%20Guide%202026%20-%20FINANCE%20&%20ACCOUNTANCY.pdf)

### Inferences
- Michael Page, Charterhouse and Cooper Fitch figures already include housing and transport. Multiplying their monthly figures by 12 approximates the fee base used in the published GCC terms. It slightly understates that base, because bonus, school fees, tickets and car value are excluded.
- If Robert Walters' figures truly exclude allowances, they understate the fee base, and fees computed from them are lower bounds.
- Fee estimates in Q4 are therefore conservative: they exclude guaranteed bonus, sign-on payments and car value, all of which the published terms include.

### Gaps
- No survey data was found on what share of GCC employers negotiate allowances out of the fee base.
- No Jordan-specific contract defining the fee base was found.

## Q4. What is the resulting typical search fee in USD for each role, and how does USD 6,000 compare?

### Takeaway
At about 20% contingency on Michael Page 2026 SME averages, typical UAE mid-market fees are:

| Role | Typical fee (USD) | USD 6,000 as share of annual pay | USD 6,000 as share of the fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finance Manager | ~19.6k | ~6.1% | ~31% |
| Financial Controller | ~26k | ~4.6% | ~23% |
| Finance Director | ~42k | ~2.8% | ~14% |
| CFO (SME) | ~49k | ~2.5% | ~12% |

In Jordan, USD 6,000 is a large share of both pay (about 10–18%) and of any percentage fee (about 50–90% of a 20% fee). That Jordan comparison rests on weak salary data and an assumed fee rate.

### Inferences
- **Share of annual pay.** In the UAE and KSA, USD 6,000 is about 1.5–7% of first-year fixed pay across the four roles. It is about 5–6% for an SME Finance Manager and about 2–2.5% for an SME CFO.
- **Share of the recruitment fee.** USD 6,000 is roughly 10–30% of a typical contingency fee for the same hire, and roughly 5–20% of a retained fee. The more senior the role, the smaller the share.
- **Effect of the fee basis.** Using the contract basis (adding guaranteed bonus, car value and similar) would raise every fee and lower every ratio. The figures above are therefore upper-bound ratios, i.e. conservative for the USD 6,000 comparison.
- **VAT.** The UAE adds 5% VAT on top of agency fees (StaffConnect, Taysir Bridge). This raises the employer's cash outlay without changing the percentages.
- **Jordan.** USD 6,000 would equal a large share of, or nearly all of, a percentage fee. Framing the USD 6,000 fee as a share of an existing recruitment fee is weak for Jordanian employers paying Jordanian salaries. It holds better where a Jordan-based employer hires at GCC-level pay.

### Gaps
- All fee percentages behind the USD fees are recruiter marketing ranges, not measured transaction averages.
- Actual average fees paid by GCC mid-market or family businesses for finance hires were not found.
- No Jordan fee percentage was found, so the 20% Jordan rate is an assumption carried over from UAE quotes, not evidence.
- Head of Finance is not separately benchmarked; it is assumed equal to Finance Director (Robert Walters combines the two).
- No guide gives a "family-owned" cut. "SME" (Michael Page: turnover below USD 50m, per the 2021 definition) is the closest proxy.

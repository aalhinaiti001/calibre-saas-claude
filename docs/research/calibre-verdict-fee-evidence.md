# USD 6,000 holds; the hours constrain it

USD 6,000 is a defensible fixed fee for the Calibre Verdict in the UAE, and the market evidence suggests it is modest rather than ambitious. For a UAE mid-market finance hire it equals roughly **8–31% of the recruiter or search fee the same employer typically pays for the same hire**. It also equals **2.4–6.1% of the hire's first-year fixed pay**. It sits at or just below the floor of published GCC advisory project fees. The constraint is internal, not external. At the USD 350 hurdle the fee buys **17.14 all-in founder hours**, and the delivery budget already spends **15.75** of them. That leaves **1.39 hours** for a live readout that is not budgeted and for selling and admin, which are budgeted at zero. If the readout takes the 1.0 hour an earlier log carried, headroom falls to **0.39 hours**. Three things limit the case. First, almost every GCC fee percentage behind the comparison is recruiter marketing, not measured transaction data. Second, the evidence breaks down for employers paying Jordanian salaries: there the fee is roughly **11–16% of a Finance Manager's annual pay**, measured on weak aggregator data. Third, the cost of a wrong hire cannot carry the price, because Calibre does not claim to predict or prevent one. The practical answer is to hold USD 6,000 through the first three Verdicts and present it against the search fee, not against a mis-hire multiplier. Budget the readout, and log enough to decide after three sales whether the problem is price or hours.

## The fee is 8–31% of the search fee for the same hire, smallest where the role is most senior

The cleanest market anchor is the fee the employer is already paying to fill the seat. No independent survey of GCC recruitment fees exists. The evidence is recruiters' own published terms plus recruiter marketing blogs, and the blogs converge on **15–25% of first-year remuneration for contingency** and **25–33% for retained search**. Most agree that retained search is invoiced in thirds ([HRnow, Jun 2025](https://www.hrnow.me/post/recruitment-agency-fees-dubai-2025); [ReapHR, Jul 2026](https://reaphr.com/blog/contingency-vs-retained-search-uae-hiring); [Mystic Sourcing, Sep 2026](https://mysticsourcing.com/blog/recruitment-agency-cost-uae)). Two primary documents back the practice. **Robert Walters' GCC retained terms** stage fees in thirds (mandate, shortlist, start date), make them non-refundable, and charge on the full package including housing, travel and other allowances; the territory explicitly covers the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Jordan, though the published version omits the fee table ([Robert Walters ToB](https://www.robertwalters.ae/content/dam/robert-walters-redesign/country/middle-east/files/terms-of-business/240807%20RW%20Dubai%20Limited%20TOB%20Retained%20(no%20fee%20table).pdf)). **Huxley's Dubai terms** print a 35% headline fee but reconcile to 18% of total remuneration, a contradiction that sits in the source itself ([Huxley UAE terms](https://www.huxley.com/media/cati1rni/6-v1-01-uae-huxley-retained-perm-recruitment-tc.pdf)).

The modal figures are therefore about 20% contingency for Finance Manager and Financial Controller hires and about 30% retained for Finance Director and CFO hires. These are recruiter-stated ranges, not measured averages.

For pay, the **Michael Page UAE Salary Guide 2026 "SME" column** is the closest match to a mid-market or family-owned employer. Earlier editions state that the monthly figures cover basic, housing and transport and define an SME as turnover below USD 50m; the 2026 edition's own footnote was not read, so whether both still apply is unverified ([Michael Page UAE 2026, Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/945892671/Salary-Guide-2026-Michael-Page-UAE); [MP 2021 definition](https://www.michaelpage.ae/sites/michaelpage.ae/files/middle_east_salary_guide_hiring_insights_2021.pdf)). Charterhouse and Cooper Fitch's SME rows broadly agree with it ([Charterhouse 2026](https://www.charterhouseme.ae/salary-levels-2026); [Cooper Fitch UAE 2025](https://cooperfitch.ae/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Salary-Guide-UAE-2025-Cooper-Fitch.pdf)).

Every row below is calculated the same way: monthly average × 12 ÷ peg, then × fee rate. For example, UAE Finance Manager: AED 30,000 × 12 = AED 360,000 ÷ 3.6725 = USD 98,026. At 20%, the contingency fee is USD 19,605, and 6,000 ÷ 19,605 = 31%.

| Role and source (monthly average) | Annual fixed pay (USD) | USD 6,000 ÷ pay | 20% contingency fee | USD 6,000 ÷ contingency fee | 30% retained fee | USD 6,000 ÷ retained fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UAE Finance Manager, MP SME (AED 30k) | 98,026 | 6.1% | 19,605 | 31% | 29,408 | 20% |
| UAE Financial Controller, MP SME (AED 40k) | 130,701 | 4.6% | 26,140 | 23% | 39,210 | 15% |
| UAE Finance Director, MP SME (AED 65k) | 212,389 | 2.8% | 42,478 | 14% | 63,717 | 9% |
| UAE CFO, MP SME (AED 75k) | 245,065 | 2.4% | 49,013 | 12% | 73,519 | 8% |
| KSA Finance Manager, MP (SAR 40k), context only | 128,000 | 4.7% | 25,600 | 23% | 38,400 | 16% |
| KSA CFO, MP (SAR 120k), context only | 384,000 | 1.6% | 76,800 | 8% | 115,200 | 5% |

KSA inputs are from [Michael Page KSA 2026](https://www.scribd.com/document/975832296/Salary-Guide-2026-Michael-Page-Saudi-Arabia). KSA is shown for context only, because delivery there is frozen while the founder is employed in the Kingdom.

These ratios are conservative in the fee's favour. The published GCC terms charge on total remuneration, including guaranteed bonus, car value and joining incentives. The salary guides exclude bonus. So real search fees are higher than shown, and the Verdict's share of them is lower. UAE agencies also add **5% VAT** on top ([StaffConnect, Aug 2026](https://www.staffconnect.ae/employment-agency-in-dubai/)), as the Verdict would. The 50/50 split on criteria lock and delivery also mirrors staging that GCC buyers already accept from search firms.

The ratios also expose a feature of a single tier: **the fee is regressive across the role range.** It weighs about two and a half times as heavily on a Finance Manager hire (6.1% of pay, 31% of the contingency fee) as on an SME CFO hire (2.4%, 12%). If price resistance appears, expect it at the Finance Manager end.

There is a steelman against the search-fee anchor. Many senior hires are made without an agency. The CIPD's 2026 UK survey puts the **median recruitment cost per senior manager or director hire at £3,500** (about USD 4,600; n=263, and only respondents claiming ±20% accuracy were counted). The private-sector senior median is £5,000 (n=170) ([CIPD Resourcing and Talent Planning 2026](https://www.cipd.org/globalassets/media/knowledge/knowledge-hub/reports/2026-pdfs/9218-resource-and-talent-planning-2026-report-web.pdf)). SHRM's 2025 US benchmarks put average cost-per-hire at **USD 5,475 non-executive and USD 35,879 executive**, from more than 2,300 members surveyed in Q1 2025 ([SHRM, Oct 2025](https://www.shrm.org/about/press-room/shrm-releases-2025-benchmarking-reports--how-does-your-organizat)). Neither survey covers the GCC. The implication still holds: for an employer hiring directly, USD 6,000 can exceed its whole recruiting spend on the role. The search-fee comparison only works for buyers who are paying a recruiter.

## Per finalist the Verdict costs USD 1,500, against about USD 1,000 for a psychologist's read

The Verdict is not psychometric and should not be sold against psychometrics. Buyers will compare it with them anyway, because assessment vendors are the nearest priced alternative. On the four finalists included in the fee, USD 6,000 is **USD 1,500 per finalist**.

The strongest comparator is a UK public-sector procurement record. North Tyneside Council pays **£750 + VAT (about USD 1,005) per shortlisted candidate** for psychologist-reviewed leadership psychometrics with a summary to the panel. That price was disclosed in June 2025 for a Chief Executive hire ([North Tyneside, Jun 2025](https://democracy.northtyneside.gov.uk/documents/s28009/Appointment%20and%20Disciplinary%20committee%20-%20Recruitment%20of%20a%20Chief%20Executive%20Head%20of%20Paid%20service.pdf)) and again in March 2026 for a Director of Resources, a senior finance role ([North Tyneside, Mar 2026](https://democracy.northtyneside.gov.uk/documents/s33590/040326%20Report%20to%20the%20Appointment%20and%20Disciplinary%20committee%20-%20Recruitment%20of%20a%20Director%20of%20Resources.pdf)). At that rate four finalists cost **USD 4,020** and six cost **USD 6,030**, almost exactly the Verdict fee.

UK boutique list prices are similar. bfpeople charges £950 + VAT (about USD 1,273) per person for psychometrics, a two-hour feedback interview and a report ([bfpeople](https://bfpeople.com/leadership-assessment-psychometric-testing/individual-leadership-assessment/)). Happy Ltd's Hogan leadership package is £835 (about USD 1,119) ([Happy Ltd, Jul 2025](https://www.happy.co.uk/leadership-and-personal-development/coaching-hub-for-leaders/1-to-1-executive-coaching-and-assessments/hogan-assessments/)). These conversions use the notes' GBP rate of 1.34.

Published GCC prices are lower per position. Iris Management Consultant (RAKEZ) lists **AED 3,500 for three "subjects" for one position** (about USD 953), each with a 60-minute interview, an assessment, a report and a debrief; "subjects" appears to mean candidates, and the page is undated ([Iris](https://www.irismanagementconsultant.com/services/talent-management-assessments)). Axiom sells an automated, non-psychologist leadership assessment at **USD 999** ([Axiom](https://axiomlabs.ae/pricing)), and TalentX's Leadership Discovery runs **USD 18,000 for up to ten leaders**, aimed at development rather than hiring ([TalentX](https://www.talentx.global/leadership-discovery)).

The higher tier, **USD 10,000–50,000+ per C-suite assessment**, is evidenced only by a search firm's marketing ([JRG Partners, Jul 2026](https://www.jrgpartners.com/executive-assessment-pricing-psychometrics-simulations-panels/)). It is an upper bound, not a benchmark.

The closer comparators are boutique fixed-fee hiring-advice offers. No GCC boutique publishes one, but undated US and UK list prices show a clear ladder. **USD 1,400** buys HR participation in a senior-hire panel with written observations ([Hire Ground HR](https://www.hiregroundhr.com/mapped-projects)); **from £2,500** (about USD 3,350) buys one-role hiring process design with scorecards ([StackBuilders](https://stackbuilderstalent.com/hiring-process-design)); **from £2,990** (about USD 4,007) buys a package that includes attending interviews ([FIG HR](https://www.fig-hr.com/recruitment/)); and **USD 4,000–12,000** buys interview design and training projects ([Miracle Recruiting](https://miraclerecruiting.com/pricing)).

USD 6,000 sits above the "sit on the panel" price point and inside the design-project band. That is where a scope with a locked rubric, blind independent scoring, a divergence review, a facilitated calibration and a written memo belongs.

The case against the fee is that a buyer can get a psychologist's read on four finalists for about two-thirds of the price. The answer is that the two products buy different things. A psychometric read prices what is learned about each candidate. The Verdict prices the discipline of the panel's own decision and a written record of it. That distinction has to be made explicitly, because the per-finalist number will be compared either way.

## At the hurdle, USD 6,000 buys 17.14 hours and the budget already spends 15.75

The internal arithmetic is the binding test, and on budget it clears. The figures come from the founder's decision log ([DECISIONS.md](../../product/DECISIONS.md)):

| Case | All-in hours | Effective rate (USD 6,000 ÷ hours) | Headroom at USD 350 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours the fee buys at the hurdle (6,000 ÷ 350) | 17.14 | 350 | 0 |
| Delivery budget as logged | 15.75 | 381 | 1.39 h |
| Budget plus 1.0 h readout (the earlier log's figure) | 16.75 | 358 | 0.39 h |
| CAL/01's earlier estimate for panel delivery | about 18 | about 333 | -0.86 h |
| The previous USD 5,500 fee against the 15.75 h budget | 15.75 | 349 | -0.04 h |

The 1.39 hours of headroom is about 8% of the hours the fee buys. A five-person panel, nine criteria, or one extra round of client questions on the memo could use all of it. Selling and admin take real time on every engagement and currently have no budget at all.

External rates support the USD 350 hurdle itself as top-of-market independent pricing, not as a Big 4 rate. In the UAE, interim CFO day rates are published at **AED 4,000–9,000** (about USD 1,089–2,451) ([FD Capital](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/dubai-cfo-recruitment/)) and senior independent advisers are quoted at **AED 5,000–10,000+ per day** (about USD 1,361–2,723+) ([Omnispay, Sep 2026](https://omnispay.com/blog/consultant-fees-uae)); USD 350/hour is USD 2,800 for an eight-hour day, at or just above the top of both bands. Across the GCC, a vendor that sells an independent-consultant network claims **USD 300–500/hour** for senior-partner-calibre independents ([Selectra Strategy](https://selectrastrategy.com/market-report/)). The one true survey is British: Holdsway (n=200, self-reported "aspirational" rates) puts the UK interim CFO median at **£1,200/day** and the 90th percentile at **£2,000** ([Holdsway, May 2026](https://www.holdsway.co.uk/blog/latest-survey-of-interim-cfo-day-rates/)), and USD 2,800 is about £2,119, just above that 90th percentile. GCC Big 4 and MBB partner rates of USD 800–2,000+/hour appear only in interested vendors' price guides ([SKYLINE, Feb 2026](https://alskyline.com/blogs/it-srv-it-consulting-firms-saudi)); they are directionally useful but should not be quoted.

On published project fees, USD 6,000 sits just under the floor of comparable advisory work. It is AED 22,035, against UAE fractional-CFO project fees of **AED 25,000–150,000+** ([CFO Centre, Mar 2026](https://www.cfocentre.com/ae/what-is-a-fractional-cfo-uae/)). It is SAR 22,500, against KSA HR project fees of **SAR 25,000–90,000** ([Talent Arabia](https://www.talentarabia.com/hr-consultancy-saudi-arabia)). Both are vendor price guides.

The pattern is that **a buyer sees a modest project fee while the founder earns a senior hourly rate**. That only holds while the hours stay near budget.

Regional vendors also describe fixed fees for a defined deliverable as the format GCC buyers trust, and hourly billing as poorly trusted ([The Growth Consultants, Apr 2026](https://thegrowthconsultants.biz/blogs/pricing-commercial-models-gcc.html); [JaZaa, 2026](https://jazaa.com/blog/fractional-cfo-cost-uae-dubai/)). These are opinions, not buyer surveys. They still support keeping the USD 350 figure internal.

## The cost of a wrong hire sets the stakes but cannot carry the price

Three popular figures fail the house rules, and none belongs in Calibre material. **"30% of first-year earnings (US Department of Labor)" is unsupported**: no DOL or BLS document containing it has been traced, and a staffing firm that markets with it now calls it "a rule of thumb, not an official statistic" ([Newman HR provenance trace](https://newmanhumanresources.com/kb/numbers/numbers-cost-of-bad-hire/); [Frontline Source Group, May 2026](https://www.frontlinesourcegroup.com/blog-us-department-of-labor-bad-hire-30-percent-of-first-year-earnings.html)). **"3x salary" is unsupported as a measured figure**: its traceable origin is a single constructed scenario in a 2017 REC report and a consultant's opinion quoted by SHRM ([REC, 2017](https://www.rec.uk.com/download_file/2883/6683); [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/real-costs-recruitment)). **"50–200% of salary" is secondary**: GCC search firms now repeat it, but it is SHRM summarising a 2006 textbook ([retainscore provenance note](https://retainscore.com/provenance/six-to-nine-months-of-salary)). Gallup's "0.5x to 2x salary" is asserted without a method ([Gallup, Mar 2019](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses-trillion.aspx)).

The measured evidence is older and smaller. The **Center for American Progress (2012)** reviewed 30 case studies from papers dated 1992–2007 and found a **median turnover cost of about 21% of annual salary** for non-executive roles; it measures replacement cost, not bad-hire cost, and only three cases are executive or physician roles ([CAP, 2012](https://www.americanprogress.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/11/CostofTurnover.pdf)). **Oxford Economics (2014)** is the only finance-specific measurement: it puts the cost of replacing a £25k+ UK accountancy employee at **"just short of £40,000"** (about USD 53,000 at today's rate, in 2014 money), mostly lost output while the replacement ramps up, with 7.4 weeks of lost output for a same-sector hire and 20.1 weeks for a hire from another sector ([Oxford Economics, 2014](https://oxfordeconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/cost-brain-drain-report.pdf)). A **CareerBuilder/Harris Poll (2017)** of US hiring managers found a self-reported average loss of **USD 14,900 per bad hire** ([PR Newswire, Dec 2017](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/nearly-three-in-four-employers-affected-by-a-bad-hire-according-to-a-recent-careerbuilder-survey-300567056.html)).

No study isolates a mis-hired CFO, Finance Director or Financial Controller, and none covers the GCC.

Applying CAP's 21% median to the UAE SME pay figures gives replacement costs of about **USD 20,585 for a Finance Manager** and **USD 51,464 for a CFO**. The fee is 29% and 12% of those amounts. That ratio states the size of the stakes. It is not a return on the fee.

Calibre does not predict performance, so it cannot claim to avert any share of that cost. Any "the fee pays for itself if it prevents X% of mis-hires" argument would be a validity claim by the back door. The fee has to stand on the decision-quality and audit-trail value of the process, priced against what the employer already spends.

GCC law moves the stakes in one specific way. A UAE employer can end a hire within the probation period of up to six months on 14 days' notice. No gratuity is due before one year of service ([Federal Decree-Law 33/2021, Arts. 9 and 51](https://dda.gov.ae/-/media/22A3CF4734034742B62468DB2721BEF1.pdf)). The sunk cost of a mis-hire is therefore mostly lost output, non-refundable permit and residency fees, and a second search fee.

The recruiter's guarantee usually does not cover that second fee. Typical guarantees are a free replacement within **12 weeks** ([Huxley UAE terms](https://www.huxley.com/media/cati1rni/6-v1-01-uae-huxley-retained-perm-recruitment-tc.pdf)) or **three months** ([HRnow, Jun 2025](https://www.hrnow.me/post/recruitment-agency-fees-dubai-2025)), so a mis-hire identified at a six-month probation review falls outside them. For a UAE SME Finance Manager, a second 20% contingency fee alone is about USD 19,605, more than three times the Verdict fee. That fact, unlike a salary multiplier, is sourced and specific to the buyer's own market.

## Most of the market evidence is recruiter marketing, and four gaps matter

The evidence has four gaps that limit how hard the case can be pushed.

**The comparison rests on recruiter marketing.** Every GCC fee percentage behind the search-fee comparison comes from recruiters marketing themselves. No AESC, CIPD or trade-body survey of GCC fees was found. A "12.5% UAE median placement fee" attributed to CIPD appears on two recruiter blogs and could not be traced to any CIPD publication ([Vantage Search Group, Sep 2026](https://vantagesearchgroup.me/insights/executive-search-fees-uae/)). If real mid-market fees cluster nearer 12–15% than 20%, the Finance Manager ratio rises from 31% to about 40–50% of the search fee.

**No GCC seller publishes a comparable product.** No GCC boutique publishes a fixed per-role hiring advisory fee. The Verdict's nearest priced comparators are US and UK.

**There is almost no data for the target segment or Jordan.** No guide gives a "family-owned" cut. Michael Page's SME column is a proxy, and its 2026 definition is unverified. No 2025–2026 recruiter salary guide covers Jordan, so Jordan pay comes from aggregators. Those aggregators are internally implausible: one shows a Finance Manager earning more than a CFO ([WorldSalaries](https://worldsalaries.com/average-finance-manager-salary-in-jordan/)). PayScale's Jordan Finance Manager figure rests on 15 profiles ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/JO/Job=Finance_Manager/Salary/2f6531ec/Amman)). No Jordan recruitment fee percentage was found at all.

**The buyer evidence describes large enterprises.** Source Global Research's GCC buyer surveys find price and speed matter more in the GCC than elsewhere, and that clients now pay premiums only for measurable value. But 94% of respondents work at organisations above USD 500m revenue, so they are not the mid-market buyers Calibre targets ([Source, GCC 2025](https://reports.sourceglobalresearch.com/report/download/9561/extract/Perceptions+of+Consulting+in+the+GCC+in+2025)). No study measures GCC SME approval thresholds, price sensitivity or invoice-currency preference for professional services.

## Jordan, the first three sales and 1.39 hours are where the price can break

**Jordan affordability is the clearest market limit.** USD 6,000 is JOD 4,254. On the weak aggregator data that is roughly **10.9–15.8% of a Jordanian Finance Manager's annual pay**, or about **1.3–1.9 months' salary**. Under an assumed 20% fee that has no Jordan evidence behind it, it is **54–79% of a recruitment fee**. A tech-focused aggregator puts local senior consultant day rates at **USD 100–350** ([consultant.dev](https://consultant.dev/jobs/jordan)), so the fee buys 17–60 local senior days. The search-fee framing that works in Dubai does not work for an Amman employer paying Amman salaries. It holds only where a Jordan-based employer hires at GCC-level pay or buys for a regional group.

**The first-three-sales risk comes more from category and credibility than from the number.** USD 6,000 is below the published floor of UAE and KSA advisory project fees, so price alone is unlikely to be the barrier with a UAE buyer who already pays a recruiter. The documented frictions lie elsewhere. In qualitative interviews (Botho, 2021, 40+ firms and six interviews), smaller UAE consultancies report that buyers' boards "strongly prefer working with a larger well-known firm" ([Consultancy-ME, Aug 2021](https://www.consultancy-me.com/news/4203/challenges-facing-uaes-small-and-mid-sized-consulting-firms)). Regional advisers also describe GCC buyers as expecting to negotiate ([The Growth Consultants, Apr 2026](https://thegrowthconsultants.biz/blogs/pricing-commercial-models-gcc.html)). Both are thin sources. Together they suggest the first buyers will test the number in negotiation, and that a discount given to win one of the first three would set the reference price for the rest. The kill criterion makes that trade-off sharper. One lost sale out of three matters more than USD 500 of margin, but a discounted price that becomes the norm also fails the hurdle on every Verdict after it.

**The hour headroom is too thin for a first engagement.** A first engagement is the one most likely to overrun: new templates, first-time client questions, and a panel learning blind scoring. If the readout takes the earlier log's 1.0 hour and the engagement needs even one hour of selling and invoicing, the effective rate falls to about **USD 338** (6,000 ÷ 17.75) and the hurdle fails. The fee clears the hurdle only on a well-run Verdict. It does not clear it on a typical first one.

KSA conditions reinforce caution rather than a higher price. A regional consulting head described a "race to the bottom" on discounting in 2025 ([FT via mirror, Mar 2025](https://abx.my.id/end-of-blank-cheque-era-for-outside-consultants-in-saudi-arabia.html?amp=1)). Government consulting payments were reported paused in May 2026, which the Ministry of Finance disputed ([The National, May 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/2026/05/21/saudi-arabia-freezes-payments-to-consultancies-as-it-feels-effects-of-iran-war/)). KSA is context only while delivery there is frozen.

## Recommendations: hold the price, budget the readout, log what decides the next move

**Hold USD 6,000 as the single list price through the first three Verdicts.** Do not discount the number. If a first buyer needs a concession, give one that leaves the list price intact and is recorded as a first-client term, for example a different payment split. Do not cut the fee. The market evidence does not call for a lower price in the UAE. Raising it before three sales would put the kill criterion at risk for evidence that does not yet exist.

**Present the fee against the search fee, in the buyer's currency, without multipliers.** The defensible sentence for a UAE prospect runs roughly like this: for a Finance Manager to CFO hire on Michael Page's 2026 SME pay averages, recruiters quote about 20% contingency or about 30% retained, which is roughly USD 19,600 (Finance Manager, contingency) to USD 73,500 (CFO, retained) per hire. The Verdict is USD 6,000, AED 22,035 or SAR 22,500, plus 5% UAE VAT where it applies (AED 23,136.75 gross). The source and the "recruiter-quoted range" limit should travel with the sentence.

Two further points make the comparison concrete. First, the 50/50 staging resembles the thirds buyers already accept from search firms. Second, a second search fee after a probation exit usually falls outside the recruiter's guarantee. Leave out salary multipliers and any suggestion that the Verdict reduces mis-hire risk.

A whole-number AED figure such as AED 22,000 would look less like a conversion against local fixed-fee packages, which are priced at round AED amounts ([BCL, Oct 2024](https://bcl.ae/blogs/how-much-does-an-accountant-cost-in-dubai/)). The USD 9.50 difference is immaterial. UAE tax invoices must show AED amounts and the exchange rate whatever the quoting currency ([FTA VATP006](https://tax.gov.ae/DataFolder/Files/Pdf/06-Tax-Invoices.pdf)).

**Qualify prospects for the first three on two tests: they are paying a recruiter, and the role is paid at GCC levels.** That puts the fee where its share of existing spend is smallest and the comparison holds. For Jordan, target employers hiring at GCC-level pay or buying for a regional group. Do not create a Jordan price until a real Jordan objection is logged. The Jordan data is too weak to set one.

**Budget the live readout at 1.0 hour now, and add an explicit selling and admin line.** That makes the budget 16.75 hours plus selling and admin against the 17.14 the fee buys. It shows plainly that the margin for selling and admin is 0.39 hours. Treat 17.14 all-in hours as a management cap, and record the reason whenever an engagement goes over it. Price the 5th and 6th finalists from the logged marginal hours per finalist at USD 350, not from the USD 1,500 average. The roughly USD 1,000 per candidate that North Tyneside pays a psychologist is a sensible outside check on that add-on price.

**Log these items on each of the first three Verdicts, then decide.**

| Log item | Why it matters for the price decision |
|---|---|
| All-in hours by phase: intake, rubric lock, scoring support, divergence review, calibration, memo, readout, follow-ups | Tests the 15.75 h budget and the 17.14 h cap phase by phase |
| Selling hours (meetings, proposal, negotiation) and admin hours (invoicing, VAT, FX, chasing) | These lines are budgeted at zero today |
| Panel size, number of finalists, number of criteria | Shows whether hours scale with scope, which would argue for scope-based add-ons rather than a higher base fee |
| Hire's package (fixed and total) and whether a recruiter was used, at what fee % | Gives Calibre its own fee-to-pay and fee-to-search-fee ratios, replacing recruiter marketing |
| Every price objection, verbatim, with the prospect's country and role level | Separates a price problem from a credibility or category problem |
| Proposals issued, won, lost, and the stated reason | The kill-criterion denominator |
| Currency requested; days to pay each 50% instalment | Tests currency friction and payment risk |
| Delivery days from criteria lock to Verdict | Tests the 10–12 business-day promise against hours |

Set three decision rules in advance. If mean all-in hours across the three Verdicts exceed 17.14, raise the fee or narrow the scope before the fourth sale, whatever the win rate. If hours sit at or under budget and no qualified UAE prospect objected on price, test USD 6,500 (the previous panel tier) on the next proposals. If two or more qualified prospects declined and cited price, review positioning before the number, because the evidence suggests the number is not the weak point.

## Conclusion

The market evidence makes USD 6,000 look cheap for what a UAE mid-market employer already spends on a finance hire, and slightly cheap against published advisory project fees. The only place it looks expensive is Jordanian pay levels, where the data is too poor to set a separate price. The real fragility is the 1.39-hour margin over the founder's own hurdle. The founder should therefore watch the first three Verdicts for overrun hours rather than for buyer resistance to the number.

The second takeaway is what the fee is anchored to. The strongest support for USD 6,000 is the search-fee comparison, built from recruiters' marketing ranges and guide salaries.

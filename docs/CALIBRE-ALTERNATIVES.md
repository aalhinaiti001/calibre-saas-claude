# Calibre without software: the alternative concepts

Prepared for Ahmad Al Hinaiti, Founder, Daftar Advisory
Date: 2 September 2026
Status: decision record, companion to the SaaS scoping concept

This document answers one question: if Calibre is not built as a SaaS, what else can it be? It uses the same evidence base as the SaaS concept and the same five lenses as the pivot strategy (capacity fit, conflict exposure, demand evidence, revenue quality, time to cash), plus a sixth, legal exposure, because the deep-research assessment showed that legal exposure is the lens that kills hiring products.

---

## 1. What any alternative has to deliver

The business overview gave Calibre one strategic job: to be the only line in the house whose delivery can be separated from the founder's hands, by template, licence, or a trained second person. The technical practice cannot scale past one person without contradicting its own promise. Calibre can. Any alternative concept is judged first on whether it does that job.

Three constraints from the pivot strategy also bind every option:

| Constraint | Effect on the choice |
|---|---|
| 8 to 12 hours per week, asynchronous, no business hours availability | Workshops, embedding and anything that needs a calendar are out. |
| KSA delivery frozen while employed; Jordan, UAE and non GCC are conflict free | The first buyers are in Jordan and the UAE regardless of concept. |
| Cash posture is generate, not invest | Every option must be cash positive from the first engagement. |

### 2.1 Licensed method kit

**What the buyer gets.** A role family pack for Financial Controller or Finance Manager: the role brief template, a weighted rubric with anchored descriptors, a blind scoring workbook that hides other panelists' scores until all are entered, a calibration guide with the written reasons discipline, the Verdict memo template in house style, the candidate notice, and the allowed and prohibited use policy. A short recorded walkthrough replaces the workshop.

**Why it works.** It delivers the strategic job completely: the method leaves the founder's hands with no software and no candidate data ever touching Daftar. Nothing in it is an instrument, so no validation claim is needed. It is the truest version of "not another ATS".

**Where it is weak.** Kits get used once and forgotten. The annual refresh and the outcome register prompt are the only hooks that bring the client back. Price discipline is also harder: a kit reads as cheaper than a person's judgment, so it must be sold as the method, not as templates.

**Indicative pricing.** Assumption, untested: one third to one half of a Verdict fee per role family pack, with the first kit sold only to a client that has already bought a Verdict.

### 2.2 Certified operator

**What it is.** A trained second person runs Verdicts under the Calibre name. The first candidate is a client's HR lead who has run the kit twice. The second is a paid associate, which the feasibility study already models at USD 50 to 55 per hour ramping from year two.

**Why it works.** It is the "trained second person" horizon from the business overview made concrete, and it is how Calibre becomes capacity additive rather than capacity competing.

**Where it is weak.** The brand carries decisions the founder did not make. That requires the use policy, the audit trail and the outcome register to be non negotiable conditions of the licence, and it needs the counsel note before the first certificate is issued.

### 2.3 Verdicts inside the Finance Desk retainer

**What it is.** D/01 gains a line: up to two finance role Verdicts per retainer year, delivered by the founder, priced inside the retainer at the USD 350 per hour benchmark rather than at a standalone discount.

**Why it works.** The deep-research assessment flagged that hiring is episodic, so a per decision product has weak repeat demand. A retainer recurs on its own. The buyer is the same CFO, the relationship already exists, and the Verdict hours are priced at the firm's best rate.

**Where it is weak.** It does nothing for the strategic job. The hours are still the founder's. It is the floor, not the concept.

### 2.4 Search firm QA layer

**What it is.** Boutique search firms in Amman and Dubai sell a Calibre Verdict on their final shortlist as a defensibility layer for their client, and as a way to reduce clawbacks on placements that fail inside the guarantee period.

**Why it works.** One channel, higher volume, and a buyer with a direct financial reason to want the memo.

**Where it is weak.** The pivot strategy deferred this with a named trigger: ten documented paid decisions with recorded outcomes. A search firm will not stake a client relationship on an unproven instrument. The Amman community is also small enough that the outreach is visible, which matters while employed.

## 3. Considered and rejected

| Idea | Why not |
|---|---|
| Finance function capability review for IPO bound companies | The prospect register's IPO readiness signals make this tempting. It assesses incumbent employees, which is the broadening the assessment rejected, and it triggers every employment law risk the Verdict avoids. |
| Paid course or handbook on finance role hiring | Zero data risk and useful for authority, but revenue is small and it does not deliver the strategic job. Keep as content, not as the concept. |
| Interviewer training for HR teams | Weaker founder authority, needs business hours, and competes head on with SHL and Mercer. |
| Any candidate facing instrument | Removed by the assessment. Not revisited here. |

---

## 4. Recommendation

**Concept 1 with concept 3 underneath it.**

The licensed method kit is the only version of Calibre that delivers the strategic job without holding candidate personal data across three data protection regimes, which is the burden the assessment said cannot yet be staffed. The retainer bundle solves the repeat demand problem and prices the founder's Verdict hours at the benchmark rate. Concept 2 is the natural sequel once a client's HR lead has run the kit twice. Concept 4 stays deferred on its existing trigger.

### How this relates to the SaaS concept

The two are not rivals. The kit is the SaaS with the software removed: same rubric, same blind scoring, same calibration discipline, same memo. If three paid Verdicts and two kit sales show that clients run the method themselves, the SaaS becomes the kit with the workbook replaced by a workspace, and the decision to build is made on evidence. If clients buy the Verdict but never run the kit, the SaaS should not be built at all, and Calibre stays a founder delivered service inside the retainer.

| Evidence after twelve months | Direction |
|---|---|
| Clients buy Verdicts and run the kit themselves | Build the SaaS per the scoping concept |
| Clients buy Verdicts, kit goes unused | Concepts 2 and 3. No software. |
| Fewer than three paid Verdicts | Kill criterion. Calibre becomes an unadvertised Daftar line item. |

---

## 5. What does not change

- **The pre conditions.** Three paid manual Verdicts, the counsel note on candidate data in Jordan and the UAE, and the allowed and prohibited use policy come first. A kit sold before the method has been run for money is a template with no evidence behind it.
- **The kill criterion.** Fewer than three paid Verdicts in twelve months ends Calibre marketing in any form, per the pivot strategy.
- **The outcome register.** It starts with the first Verdict, whatever the concept, because it is the only thing that ever earns Calibre the right to a benchmark claim.
